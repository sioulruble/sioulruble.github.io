Hi, I'm Louis, a first-year PhD candidate at Sorbonne University , supervised by Catherine Pelachaud, inside the ACIDE team at ISIR. My research focuses on interruption, turn-taking, and backchannels in socio-emotional dialogue agents, exploring how conversational systems can perceive, anticipate, and adapt to the dynamics of social interaction. Broadly, I combine deep learning, multimodal perception, and computational modelling to study the cognitive mechanisms underlying human communication.

What fascinates me most in my field is the Media Equation (Reeves & Nass): people tend to treat machines as social partners, applying to them the same expectations they hold towards humans. This raises the question at the heart of my research: since the way an agent takes the floor, interrupts, or gives feedback already says something about who it seems to be, can we modulate the personality people attribute to it across several modalities, i.e. speech, gaze, gesture, facial expression, and timing, and even across different platforms?

Publications: coming soon, my first papers are in preparation.

Studying the history of technics helps me understand how the tools we build reshape the way we live and work together. I see social robots as heirs of the Industrial Revolution, which introduced machines to reduce human labour and increase productivity, standardising work in the process. Social robots extend this logic to interaction itself: if we are not careful, they could end up standardising the way we relate to one another. Drawing on Bernard Stiegler’s work on how industrial technologies can erode human know-how, I question not only what technology makes possible, but also what kinds of relationships it encourages us to develop.
